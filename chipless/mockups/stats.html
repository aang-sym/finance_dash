Mockup 06 · end of night

Who owes whom, and what happened.

Two jobs on one screen. The urgent one is money: get four people paid up with the fewest possible taps at 1am. The second is the reason anyone comes back — what actually happened over 47 hands.

  1. Debt minimisation, not a matrix. Four players' balances naively imply up to six transfers. Netting them off gives three, and the header says so, because "3 payments, not 6" is the feature. The incumbent has a Settle Up screen; this is where it's easy to be much better than it.
  2. Net position, not stack size. Plotting stacks would make Jony's rebuy look like he suddenly won $50. Net profit against a zero line is the number people actually argue about, and it makes the zero line meaningful.
  3. The chart palette is not the chip palette. The chip colours fail as line series — pine and plum fall below the chroma floor and read gray, and pine↔oxblood is ΔE 6.0 under protanopia. These four were stepped until the checks passed. Colour is bound to the person, so filtering a player out never repaints the others.
  4. Every series is directly labelled at its end and the results table sits right above, so identity is never carried by colour alone. That's also what discharges the one remaining contrast warning on Sam's ochre at 2.35:1.
  5. Stats compare against real Hold'em frequencies. "You won with two pair" is trivia; "three times more often than the maths says" is a thing you send to the group chat. The accent tick on each bar is the true frequency.
  6. All of it is paid for by one tap per hand — the declare prompt on the showdown screen. No extra data entry anywhere, which is the only reason people will actually do it.
The settle-up maths

Nets: You +26, Mike +24, Sam −12, Jony −38. Greedily match the largest debtor to the largest creditor, subtract, repeat: Jony→You $26, Jony→Mike $12, Sam→Mike $12. Three transfers for four players, and it generalises to n−1 worst case.

Computed server-side in integer cents alongside the pot maths — the same reason the client never holds a balance.
