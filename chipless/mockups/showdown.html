Mockup 04 · showdown

The payout, frozen mid-rake.

This is the emotional peak of the whole app and deserves disproportionate effort. It's also the one place where a static mockup can only get you halfway — the point of this moment is timing and sound, so treat the frame below as one still from a four-second sequence.

  1. The chips travel as a loose sheaf, not a rigid line. Sampled along a bezier from the pot to your rail, each chip jittered off the path and rotated to its tangent, scaling up slightly as it comes toward you. A tidy queue of chips looks like a progress bar; a sheaf looks like money being pushed.
  2. Everything except the money desaturates. The pot above is still visible at 28% saturation with $4 left in it, so you can see the rake actually draining it. Colour is the cheapest possible way to point the eye.
  3. The count-up shows where you came from. $40 → $76 with the struck-through original. Balatro's payouts land partly because you watch the number climb rather than arriving at it.
  4. Sound scales with the pot. A $4 pot is a handful of clinks; this $36 pot is a multi-second granular cascade. The winner's device gets the long version, everyone else a shorter duller thud — the asymmetry is the reward.
  5. "What'd you have?" is a poker ritual, not a form. Ten tiles, one tap, skippable, and the ranks actually live on this board are pulled forward. That single tap is what makes every stat on the end-of-night page possible.
  6. Deal next hand is the only primary action. Rebuys and Settle up are deliberately quiet — at 1am nobody wants to hunt for the button that keeps the game moving.
The sequence this frame sits inside
  1. Beat of silence. Everything but the pot desaturates.
  2. The rake — chips leave as a sheaf, table kicks 4px on release.
  3. This frame. Cascade audio, length scaled to $36.
  4. Stack counts up on a rising pitch ladder, then settles.
  5. Declare prompt slides in. Never before the count-up finishes.
