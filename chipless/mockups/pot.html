Mockup 03 · pot inspector

Leaning over the table.

Tapping the pot doesn't open a sheet — the camera moves. The pot ellipse from the table view grows to fill the screen and the chips already in it keep their identity, spreading apart as the camera arrives. Everything else stays exactly where it was, pushed back and dimmed.

  1. The table is visibly still behind. Chrome at the top and the rail at the bottom are the real elements, scaled back, blurred and desaturated rather than replaced. A modal would cover them; a camera move keeps them, and that's the whole difference in how it feels.
  2. Chips are grouped by who put them in, in wedges, with a pill on each. You can see the pot is mostly three people at $10 each — that's a read you cannot get from a number.
  3. Side pots are concentric, and the geometry does the explaining. The main pot is the inner ellipse because everyone is in it. The side pot is the ring outside it, and Sam simply has no chips out there — he's all-in for less. Nobody has to read a legend to see who can win what.
  4. Your own wedge is at the bottom, nearest your hands, and inverted so you find it instantly. Every player's device puts them at the bottom.
  5. Scatter is seeded on (handId, actionIndex) so this pile is identical on all four phones, down to the pixel. Random-per-client would quietly break the premise that everyone is looking at the same table.
  6. The log scrubs horizontally and the current state is the dark chip on the end. Dragging back through it rewinds the pot diagram — how the money actually accumulated.
The side-pot maths, since it has to be right

Sam is all-in for $6. Jony, Mike and you are in for $10 each. The main pot caps at Sam's stake: 4 × $6 = $24. The remainder sits in a side pot Sam can't win: 3 × $4 = $12. Total $36.

All of this is computed server-side in integer cents and broadcast — the client only ever draws what it's told. Never trust a client with a balance.
