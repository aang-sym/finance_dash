A tactile chipless poker app.

Six static pages exploring a rebuild of playchipless.com around the thing it throws away: the feel of chips. No framework, no interactivity beyond one chart tooltip — these exist to settle the art direction and the screen architecture before any code gets written. Design reasoning lives in the annotations beside each screen.

  1. 01 Chip lab Four art directions at true size — screen-printed, banknote intaglio, board-game token, 1-bit dither. Full denomination sets, edge-on stacks, pot clusters, and a greyscale legibility strip per direction. This is the decision to make first. →
  2. 02 Table The hero screen. Pot at the top, your rail at the bottom, four chips built above the betting line. Betting is a gesture, not a number field. →
  3. 03 Pot inspector The zoom — a camera move, not a modal. Contributions in wedges, side pots as concentric rings, and the payout preview the geometry implies. →
  4. 04 Showdown The payout frozen mid-rake, chips travelling as a loose sheaf. One still from a four-second sequence — the annotations lay out the other beats. →
  5. 05 Landing Sells the feel rather than the bookkeeping, and takes the join code inline so the guest path is one screen instead of two. →
  6. 06 End of night Settle-up netted down to the fewest payments, plus the session in numbers — all paid for by one optional tap per hand. →
How to read these

Every page is self-contained apart from chips.js, which holds the chip factory — denominations, the four art directions, edge-on stacks, and the seeded pot scatter. One source of truth, written to lift into the real app.

App screens are drawn at 390 × 844 with review notes alongside; below 430px wide they go full-bleed so you can open them on an actual phone, which is the only way to judge them properly. The landing page is a real responsive page.
