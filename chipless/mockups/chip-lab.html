Four ways to draw money.

Six denominations, four art directions, rendered at the size a thumb actually sees them. The rule throughout: these are tokens, not casino chips — photoreal clay reads as mud at 44px, and "casino" is the wrong register for a kitchen table. Every denomination is coded three ways at once (value, edge pattern, numeral) so it survives a dim room and a colourblind eye. Hit greyscale to check that claim.

Where I land

A, with B's rosette as the face art. A's flat silhouette and hard offset shadow survive being small, which is the only test that really matters here — and the grain is what stops it looking like default vector output. But A's centre is the weakest part of it: a numeral in a circle is the one genuinely generic move on this sheet. Dropping B's guilloché in there fixes it and pulls the whole thing toward money rather than gambling, which is the more interesting place for this product to sit.

Two honest notes against my own recommendation:

  1. C is the one I'd bet on lasting longest. No numerals at all, denomination by notch and dot count — it's the quietest and it's the only direction that doesn't lean on poker iconography. If you want this to feel like an object rather than an app, it's C.
  2. D's coding is the most elegant on the sheet — dither density is the denomination, so the value ladder and the art style are the same decision. It also passes greyscale trivially, because it's already greyscale. It's the riskiest at six denominations and it forces a dark UI, but it's the most distinctive thing here by a distance.

B alone I'd rule out as a full direction: the hairlines choke below about 80px, which you can see in the true-size row. It earns its place as a detail layer, not a system.
